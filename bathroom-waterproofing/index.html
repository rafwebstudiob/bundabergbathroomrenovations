Bathroom waterproofing in Bundaberg

In short: Waterproofing is one of the most important parts of a bathroom renovation. Wet areas must meet the National Construction Code and AS 3740, and you should get a certificate when the work is done.

What to know and what to ask, with links to official sources.

Last updated: 24 November 2026

This guide explains why waterproofing matters and the official rules that apply in Bundaberg. It is not technical or legal advice; the licensed trades and renovator are responsible for compliant work.

Why waterproofing matters

Water escaping through a shower or bathroom floor can rot framing, damage walls and ceilings, and lead to mould, often long before you see it. Failed waterproofing is expensive to fix because tiles and fixtures have to come out. That's why it is one of the most important steps in a bathroom renovation, even though you never see it once the tiles are on.

The standard

The National Construction Code requires wet areas in homes to be waterproofed, and the Australian Standard for waterproofing domestic wet areas is AS 3740. Ask the renovator which parts of the room will be waterproofed and to what height, and what products and system they use.

Licensing in Queensland

In Queensland, wet-area waterproofing must meet the National Construction Code and AS 3740. The work should be done by a contractor whose QBCC licence covers it, and plumbing work must be done by a licensed plumber. Ask for a waterproofing certificate when the membrane is finished, and keep it with the quote and warranties.

Source: QBCC, Domestic building contracts.

Questions to ask

  • Who will do the waterproofing, and what licence do they hold?
  • Will I get a waterproofing certificate when it's done?
  • How long does the membrane cure before tiling?
  • Is a flood test or inspection part of the process?
  • What warranty covers the waterproofing?

Older bathrooms in Bundaberg

Inner Bundaberg has older timber Queenslanders and post-war homes, family suburbs such as Avoca, Kepnock and Ashfield have brick and later houses, and Bargara, Innes Park and Burnett Heads mix older beach houses with newer coastal homes. Older bathrooms were often built before current waterproofing standards, so a renovation is a good time to bring the wet areas up to date.
